A purchase order is a commitment. Once money moves, your options shrink. The cheapest time to find a problem with a supplier is before you issue one.

## What to check
- **Legal existence.** The business is registered and the details match its paperwork.
- **Track record.** Recent, relevant work you can confirm with the people it was done for.
- **Capacity.** The supplier can deliver your volume on your timeline, not only in general.
- **Documents.** Licences, certificates and tax records are current.
- **Financial standing.** Enough stability to finish the job without asking for more money midway.

## When to check
Do the checks after a supplier makes your shortlist and before any commitment. It is a short step, and it protects the rest of the project.

## What it saves
A failed delivery costs more than the goods. It delays the whole project, and you carry the delay. A few days of verification is usually far cheaper.

Agree the checks for each engagement, and keep the records. They are also useful evidence later.
